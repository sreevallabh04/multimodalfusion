+\subsubsection{Statistical testing}
+\label{sec:stat_testing}
+Pairwise algorithmic comparisons on a \emph{fixed} test partition violate the independence assumptions of na\"{\i}ve two-sample $t$-tests on scalar accuracies because each exemplar contributes paired outcomes. Consequently, discordant prediction patterns between two trained models $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ were evaluated using \textbf{McNemar's test} applied to paired correct/incorrect indicators on identical test exemplars ($n_{\mathrm{test}} \approx \lceil 0.15 \cdot 838\rceil = 126$ under the stratified $15$\% MangoFruitDDS partition). Whenever the number of discordant pairs fulfilled standard regularity assumptions, the asymptotic $\chi^{2}$ implementation with continuity correction was used; otherwise the exact binomial variant on discordant counts was consulted following standard paired-classifier practice \cite{dietterich1998}. Inference was assessed at two-sided significance $\alpha = 0.05$, reporting the McNemar $p$-value comparing the null hypothesis that both models disagree symmetrically versus the paired alternative hypothesis.
+

This gauges stability of ROC-based gains under observational perturbation complementary to classification-error symmetry tested by McNemar\@. All computations were scripted in Python~3.x using SciPy and scikit-learn, mirroring the released evaluation helper script \texttt{statistical\_tests.py}.
